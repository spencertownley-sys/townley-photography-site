Case-study-style video: why it outperforms a traditional ad

A case-study video, a real customer explaining a real result in their own words, outperforms a traditional ad for a simple reason: nobody believes ads, and almost everybody believes a specific story told plainly. It is the difference between a company claiming and a customer confirming.

These are my favorite marketing films to make around Seattle and Tacoma, partly because they work and partly because the ingredients are just true things.

Why belief beats reach

A traditional ad rents attention and spends it on a claim the viewer discounts automatically, because the company said it about itself. A case study borrows credibility instead: the customer has no obvious reason to lie, their details are checkable, and their hesitations on camera read as honesty rather than bad acting. Fewer people may watch it than a splashy ad, but the ones who do arrive far closer to a decision.

The structure that works

Three beats, always. The problem: what was broken, costing, or frustrating, told with enough texture that your prospect recognizes themselves. The search: what they tried, why they chose you, said in their words rather than your tagline. The result: what changed, as specifically as they are willing to be. Numbers are wonderful when real; "I stopped worrying about it" is nearly as convincing and always available. Keep the whole film under three minutes and the best cut under one.

Specificity is the engine

Generic praise ("great service, highly recommend") evaporates on contact. Specifics stick: the deadline that got hit, the problem nobody else would touch, the detail that surprised them. In the edit I keep the pauses and the imperfect phrasing, because sanded-smooth testimonial language sounds exactly like the ads nobody believes.

Production notes, briefly

Shoot in the customer's real environment, not a studio; the location is evidence too, and it puts the customer at ease in a way no studio can. Prioritize audio with a lav mic, ask open questions, and never hand anyone a script. B-roll of the actual work or product does the connective tissue. A half day on site usually covers it, which also makes this one of the most affordable films per unit of persuasion.

One story, many assets

A single case-study shoot yields the full film, a sixty-second cut for the website, and vertical clips for social, because the strongest twenty seconds of a real person talking is already a complete ad. That multiplication is what makes the format efficient as well as persuasive, and it is why I plan the cutdowns before the interview, not after.

Where it earns its keep

Sales conversations, where one link answers "does this actually work"; the website, next to the service it proves; and retargeting, where warm audiences meet it at exactly the right moment of doubt. The honest caveat: you need a customer with a real result who will say so on camera. If you have that customer in mind, tell me about them here, or see film work on the video page.
