What to look for in an event videographer in Seattle

Hiring an event videographer in Seattle comes down to four things: full-length samples of their work, a plan for your specific event, deliverables in writing, and a serious answer about audio. Everything else is preference and price.

I shoot event films across the greater Seattle and Tacoma area, and I have also been the person hiring crews, so this list works from both sides of the table.

Watch full films, not just reels

A highlight reel shows the best forty seconds a videographer has ever shot. A full event film shows judgment: pacing, coverage of the boring-but-required moments, how they handle bad light, hard rooms, and a schedule running forty minutes late. Ask for two or three complete deliverables from events like yours, ideally shot in venues like yours. Anyone proud of their work will send them quickly, and hesitation is itself an answer.

Ask about audio and listen closely

Audio is the divide between professional and amateur event video. Speeches, vows, toasts, panel discussions: if the words matter, the videographer needs a plan beyond the camera's built-in microphone, usually lav mics, a recorder on the soundboard, and backups of both. If the answer to "how will you capture the speeches" is vague, the footage will be too, and no edit rescues bad audio.

Get deliverables and turnaround in writing

Exactly what do you receive: a full-length edit, a highlight film, social cutdowns, raw footage or not, and delivered when. My video projects run a one week turnaround, stated up front, because "when it is ready" is not a delivery date. Whoever you hire, the deliverables list belongs in the agreement, not in your assumptions.

Coverage, backup, and boring reliability

Ask how many shooters your event actually needs; a single skilled operator covers most single-room programs, while multi-room events, simultaneous moments, or anything with one unrepeatable moment argue for a second camera. Ask what happens if a camera fails or the videographer is sick. Professionals have answers: dual card recording, spare bodies, a backup shooter they can call. Ask about insurance too; many Seattle venues require a certificate, and a pro will have sent one before.

Red flags, briefly

No full-length samples, only sizzle. Vague answers about audio or backup gear. No written deliverables list. A price dramatically below the market, which usually means a single camera, no redundancy, and an edit measured in months. And anyone who cannot explain their plan for YOUR event, rather than events in general, is planning to improvise with your one chance at the footage.

The fit question

Event video is hours of a person moving through your guests. My rule for every shoot is that if we are not having fun, we are doing it wrong; a relaxed crew reads as invisible, and invisible is what you want in the room. If you have an event coming up, tell me about it and check availability here; you can see event and film work on the video page.
