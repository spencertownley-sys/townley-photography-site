How to prep a home for photos before the photographer arrives

The best things a seller can do before a real estate photo shoot are simple: clear the counters, hide the cords, and turn on every light in the house. Thirty minutes of prep changes the photos more than any editing can.

This is the checklist I send with every listing booking in the Seattle and Tacoma area. Agents, feel free to forward it verbatim.

The whole-house pass

Turn on every light, including lamps and under-cabinet strips, and replace any dead bulbs. Open blinds and curtains to matching heights. Hide visible cords, chargers, and remotes. Clear personal photos if privacy matters to the seller, and put shoes, mail, and pet gear behind closed doors. The camera sees everything at once in a way the eye does not, and a stray laundry basket will outlast the listing in every saved screenshot.

Kitchen

Counters as close to bare as the seller can stand: a coffee maker or a bowl of fruit reads as staging, six appliances read as clutter. Clear the refrigerator door completely. Hide dish racks, sponges, and towels, and push chairs in evenly at the table.

Bathrooms

Nothing on the vanity except perhaps a soap dispenser. Toilet lids down, every time. Fresh towels hung straight, shower doors squeegeed, personal products into a drawer for an hour. Bathrooms photograph smaller than they feel, so every visible object costs space.

Bedrooms and living areas

Beds made tight with the duvet pulled square, since bedding wrinkles are the most common flaw in listing photos. Nightstands down to a lamp and a book. In living spaces, straighten pillows, fold throws, and pull furniture a few inches off the walls if the room allows; it photographs more open, not less.

Outside

Cars out of the driveway and off the street in front. Bins, hoses, and yard tools out of sight, lawn mowed, and the porch swept. If aerials are booked, remember the backyard is in frame too: patio furniture arranged, cushions on, cover off the grill.

Pets and people

Pets should be out of the house or crated somewhere off the shot list, and their bowls, beds, and litter boxes tucked away; buyers with allergies read those as a warning label. The same goes for people: an empty house shoots faster and photographs cleaner, so plan the session for a window when the family can be out for an hour.

What not to worry about

Angles, lighting, and small imperfections are my job, and minor things like an outlet cover or a garden hose can be handled on site or in editing. Prep is about the stuff no photographer can fix: a counter full of appliances is a counter full of appliances from every angle. Get the checklist done and the shoot runs fast, usually about an hour, with photos back within 48 hours. Booking a listing shoot is one short form away.
