A season-by-season guide to photography in the Pacific Northwest

The Pacific Northwest gives photographers four genuinely different seasons, and knowing what each one does best is most of the planning battle. This is the calendar I actually shoot by, from spring at Green Lake to winter dusk at Kerry Park.

It applies whether you are booking a session or carrying your own camera; the light does not check receipts. Each season below gets the same treatment: what it does best, where it does it, and the one honest caveat that comes with it.

Spring: drama for free

Spring here is unstable skies, and unstable skies are a gift: broken clouds after rain produce the most dramatic light of the year, and everything is saturated and green. Green Lake leafs out beautifully for portraits, and the Cascades serve storm light for landscapes. Plan flexibly, keep a rain date, and treat a mixed forecast as a feature rather than a threat. Wildlife wakes up too; spring mornings are the year's busiest. The one honest warning: spring will change its mind mid-session, which is why layers made the wardrobe guide.

Summer: the long golden evenings

Summer is reliability: dry weeks, sunsets past 9pm, and golden hour sessions that do not require anyone to skip dinner. Lincoln Park's west-facing beach is the signature summer location, with true sunsets over the Sound. It is also peak season for real estate exteriors and events, so calendars fill earliest; book summer dates ahead of the season, not inside it. The one caution is midday: high summer sun is the harshest light of the year, so mornings and evenings do the real work.

Fall: the overachiever

Fall is the strongest all-around season. Color turns Green Lake into the classic warm portrait backdrop, crisp air gives Kerry Park its early city-light dusks, and the first storms bring the landscape drama back. Light gets warmer and lower all day, not just at the edges. If you can only book one outdoor session a year, October is my honest recommendation.

Winter: short, moody, underrated

Winter compresses the day, dusk at 4:30pm, and that is the trick to using it: city skylines light up early, overcast light is soft and even for close portraits, and fog does things to treelines that summer cannot. Clear cold evenings give the sharpest city views of the year, with air the summer haze never allows. Fewer people book winter, which means flexible scheduling and empty locations, a trade worth knowing about if crowds bother you more than cold does.

Planning around all of it

Match the session to the season rather than fighting the calendar: families in fall color, couples on summer beaches, moody portraits and city dusk in winter, everything with a weather plan in spring. Location and timing guidance comes with every portrait booking, photos deliver within 48 hours year-round, and the weather policy is simple: reschedules cost nothing when the sky overrules us.
