Best spots for couples portraits in Seattle, season by season

The best couples portrait locations in Seattle change with the season, and the difference matters more here than in most cities. These are the spots I actually use through the year, mainly Green Lake, Lincoln Park, and Kerry Park, and when each one earns its place.

Anniversary sessions, engagements, or no occasion at all: the location advice is the same. A good location does two jobs at once. It gives the photos a backdrop worth looking at, and it gives the two of you something to do besides stand there being photographed, which is where the real expressions come from.

Spring

Green Lake is the spring pick. The trees leaf out, the light through them goes soft and green, and the path gives you endless backdrops within a short walk. Spring here also means unpredictable skies, which I consider a feature: broken clouds after rain produce some of the most dramatic light of the year, and the crowds have not arrived yet.

Summer

Lincoln Park on a summer evening is as good as this city gets. A west-facing beach on Puget Sound, driftwood to sit on, madrona trees along the bluff, and a sunset that lands straight in front of you. Summer sunsets run late, past 9pm in June and July, so these are relaxed evening sessions. Bring a layer for when the wind comes off the water, and plan to stay through the last light. That final stretch is the whole point.

Fall

Fall splits the vote. Green Lake turns over into full color and is the obvious choice for warm, classic sessions. But fall is also when Kerry Park starts making sense again: sunset comes early enough that we can catch the skyline at dusk without it being a late night. Crisp air, early lights, city behind you. It photographs like a movie poster, and I mean that as a compliment.

Winter

Winter is Kerry Park season. Dusk arrives at 4:30pm, the skyline lights up, and the overlook is nearly empty on a weekday. Cold, clear winter evenings give you the sharpest city views of the year. And when the weather refuses to cooperate, overcast winter light is soft and even, genuinely good for close portraits. Bundle up and lean in; coats and scarves photograph beautifully.

The rain plan

Every couples session here needs a weather conversation, so let us have it now. Light drizzle photographs better than you would expect: umbrellas, wet pavement reflections, and a reason to stand close are not exactly obstacles for couples photos. Heavy rain means we move the date, and I make that call with you a day or two out rather than gambling your evening on it. Deposits carry over when weather forces a reschedule, so there is no penalty for living in a city with opinions about your plans.
