Golden hour in Seattle: when and where to shoot for the best light

Golden hour in Seattle is roughly the last hour before sunset, when the light goes warm, low, and flattering. Because our sunset moves from around 4:20pm in midwinter to after 9pm in late June, the right time to shoot shifts by almost five hours across the year.

That swing matters more here than in most cities, and it is the first thing I check before booking any outdoor session.

When it actually happens

In summer, plan on an evening session: the good light starts around 7:30 or 8pm and holds until past 9. In spring and fall it lands conveniently around dinner time. In winter, golden hour is mid-afternoon, which sounds wrong until you realize you can shoot beautiful low light at 3:30pm and still be home for dinner. Winter light here, when it shows up, is some of the best of the year. There is also a bonus round most people skip: the twenty minutes after sunset, when the sky goes soft blue and the city lights come on. If your photographer suggests staying a little past sunset, stay.

Where to use it

Three spots I return to constantly. Green Lake takes evening light beautifully: it filters through the trees on the west side and bounces soft off the water, and there is always a stretch of path that has the sun exactly where I want it. Lincoln Park in West Seattle is the true sunset spot, with a west-facing beach looking straight across Puget Sound, driftwood, and that last ten minutes when the whole Sound goes amber. Kerry Park earns its reputation just after the sun drops, when the sky still holds color and the city lights come up behind you. Technically that is blue hour, and it is worth staying for.

The overcast asterisk

Some weeks, Seattle simply declines to provide a sunset. This is less of a problem than people think. An overcast sky is a giant softbox: even, gentle light with no harsh shadows and no squinting, and it is arguably better for portraits than direct sun. Golden hour is the dessert, not the meal. I do not cancel sessions for clouds, and you should not either.

The hour to avoid

If golden hour is the best light of the day, midday sun is the worst: overhead, harsh, and hard on faces, with shadows in eye sockets and everyone squinting. On a rare bright Seattle day I will move a midday session into open shade and it works fine, but if we have the choice, we take the evening. The one exception is families with small kids, where nap schedules outrank light. A rested toddler at noon beats a meltdown at sunset, and I plan accordingly.
