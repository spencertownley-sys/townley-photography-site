Meet Spencer Townley: a Puget Sound photographer's story

I am Spencer Townley, a photographer and filmmaker based in the Puget Sound area, shooting portraits, real estate, landscape and wildlife, and film for clients ranging from individuals and families to Fortune 500 companies. The whole thing started with disposable cameras at the zoo.

Since a post like this is really an introduction, here is the honest version of how it happened and how I work.

The zoo years

As a kid I burned through disposable cameras at the zoo, fully convinced I was one good elephant photo away from National Geographic. The magazine never called. The habit stayed: the waiting, the watching, the small victory of an animal doing something interesting while you happened to be ready. Most of what I know about patience I learned standing in front of enclosures with 24 exposures and no do-overs.

Video found me early

The moving image arrived through short movies made as a kid, the kind with production values best described as enthusiastic. That grew into shooting commercials for Gonzaga University during college, which taught me that film work is logistics wearing an art costume, and continued through my career at Starbucks. Ten-plus years behind cameras of every kind later, the photo side and the video side stopped being separate skills and became one job: noticing things and being ready.

Why I shoot broadly on purpose

Townley Photography covers portraits, real estate, landscape and wildlife prints, event film, and marketing video across the greater Seattle and Tacoma area. That breadth is a choice, not indecision: the same eye that lights a family portrait frames a listing, a ridgeline, or a brand film, and every discipline sharpens the others. Real estate teaches speed, wildlife teaches patience, and portraits teach people, which is the hardest and best part of all of it.

The rule I run every shoot by

If we are not having fun, we are doing it wrong. The best results come when everyone is relaxed, and it is my job to make that happen: for a nervous family at Green Lake, an agent on a deadline, or a founder who hates being on camera. Relaxed people photograph like themselves. Tense people photograph like hostages, and no lens fixes that.

The places that keep pulling me back

Around home: Green Lake, Kerry Park, and Lincoln Park, each of which earns its place in a different season. Further out: the trip that reset my standards was a safari through South Africa, Namibia, Botswana, and Zimbabwe, sleeping in camps on elephant trails, waking to lions, drinking coffee above a hippo pond. I came home with full cards and a permanent grudge against alarm clocks that are not lions.
