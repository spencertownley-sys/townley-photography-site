How much should a small business budget for a brand video?

Most small businesses can produce a strong brand video for somewhere between a few thousand dollars and the low five figures, and the spread depends mostly on shoot days and editing scope. The useful question is not the number; it is what moves the number.

I produce marketing video for businesses around Seattle and Tacoma, and the budget conversation is the first one we have. Here is how I walk clients through it.

The question before the budget

Before any number: what is this video supposed to make happen? "Look established" and "book consultations" are different films with different costs. A clear job description for the video keeps the budget honest, because every line item either serves the goal or decorates it.

What actually drives cost

Four levers, in order of weight. Shoot days: a second day of filming costs nearly as much as the first, so a one-day plan is the single biggest saving. Locations: every move eats an hour of light and setup. Concept: interviews and real b-roll are efficient; scripted scenes with actors are not. And editing scope: one two-minute film is one price, while a film plus six social cutdowns is another, though cutdowns from footage you already own are the cheapest content you will ever buy.

Ranges without the fog

Loosely, and every market varies: a single-day, one-location brand film with an interview backbone and real b-roll sits at the lower end of that few-thousand-dollar range, and it is where most first brand videos should start. Multi-day productions with scripting, actors, or multiple locations climb fast. Anyone quoting a precise number before asking what you are trying to say is quoting their availability, not your project.

Where a small budget goes furthest

One day, one location, one message, real people. The owner talking honestly about why the business exists, cut with footage of the work actually happening, beats a bigger production with nothing to say. Spend on good audio and a clear story before anything else; viewers forgive modest visuals and never forgive mumble or waffle.

Where budgets get wasted

Trying to say everything in one video, which produces a long film nobody finishes. Reshoots caused by skipping the planning call, which are always more expensive than the planning call. Licensing an expensive song when a well-chosen library track does the job. And chasing cinematic flourishes before the fundamentals, story, audio, light, are paid for.

How I run it

We settle the message and the deliverables list first, then the price is fixed before anyone touches a camera: a 25 percent deposit holds the production dates, refundable with a week of notice, and video projects run a one week turnaround after the shoot. If you are budgeting a brand video, tell me about the business here or see film work on the video page; I will tell you honestly what your budget buys, including when the answer is "wait and save."
