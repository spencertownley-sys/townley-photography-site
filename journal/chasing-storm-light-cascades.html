Chasing storm light in the Cascades

Most people pack up and head home when the forecast turns. That's usually the moment worth staying for. Storm light, the gap between a squall clearing and the next one rolling in, produces contrast and drama that a clear-sky day never will.

Reading the window

The shot isn't during the storm, it's in the ten or fifteen minutes after, when broken cloud lets shafts of light through onto wet rock and moving fog. Watching the radar and knowing the terrain matters more than any piece of gear.

Practically: keep the camera dry until the window opens, keep both hands free to move fast, and expect to come home with far more misses than keepers. The ones that land are worth the wait.
