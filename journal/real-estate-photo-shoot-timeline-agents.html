What to expect during a real estate photo shoot: a timeline for agents

A standard real estate photo shoot takes about 60 to 90 minutes on site for a typical home, and the finished gallery is delivered within 48 hours. Here is the full timeline I walk agents through, so you can slot a shoot into a listing schedule without guessing.

I photograph listings across the greater Seattle and Tacoma area, and the process below is the same one every time. Predictability is most of what agents are paying for.

Before I arrive

The house should be photo-ready when I walk in: staged, decluttered, lights on. I send a one-page prep sheet at booking so the seller knows exactly what that means. I also confirm access, check the weather window if aerials are booked, and look at the listing's orientation to plan which side of the house gets the best light at our scheduled time.

The first fifteen minutes

I start with a quick walkthrough, noting the rooms that sell the house and anything that needs a small fix: a trash can to hide, a rug to straighten, blinds set to matching heights. Then I shoot the money rooms first, while the light is doing what I planned for. Kitchen and main living space almost always lead.

The middle hour

Interiors, room by room, tripod-mounted for straight verticals and consistent exposure. Windows get balanced so the view reads instead of blowing out white, which is one of the clearest differences between professional work and a phone photo. Bathrooms, bedrooms, and detail shots follow, and I finish inside with anything the listing description highlights: a fireplace, a pantry, a view corner.

The aerial pass, if booked

Aerials add roughly fifteen minutes at the end: an establishing shot, a lower orbit, and a straight-down frame for lot clarity. I am FAA Part 107 licensed and handle any airspace authorization before arrival, so this step never stalls the schedule. Wind or rain can ground the flight, in which case I return for the aerials rather than skip them.

After I leave

Editing happens same-day or next-day: exposure, color, verticals, sky where honest. The finished gallery arrives within 48 hours, sized for MLS and ready for print, with aerials included in the same delivery. If your listing goes live Thursday, we shoot Monday or Tuesday and you are never waiting on photos.

Scheduling and lead time

Most listing shoots book within the same week, and I hold morning and midday slots for interiors since that is when window light behaves. Twilight exteriors, when a listing warrants them, are a separate short visit at dusk. A 25 percent deposit holds the slot, refundable with a week of notice, which matters in a business where listing dates move.

How agents can make it fast

Two things move the needle: a seller who has actually done the prep, and a confirmed empty house, since occupants in frame slow everything down. Get those right and the whole visit runs closer to an hour. If you want the prep sheet ahead of your next listing, send an inquiry here and I will include it with the booking confirmation.
